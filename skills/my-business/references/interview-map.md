# Interview coverage map

Ask one question at a time. Skip information already supported by files or reliable user-provided sources. Required topics come first. Optional topics require consent.

## 1. Identity and purpose

- Person's preferred name and how Claude should address them
- Business, company, or brand names
- One-sentence description of what they do
- Working languages and primary markets
- Public website and social profiles
- Why the business exists and what the user believes

## 2. Offers

For each active product or service:

- Name and plain-language description
- Intended customer and problem solved
- Delivery model and current price, if useful
- Public link
- Current status, constraints, refund or fulfillment rules

Separate active, planned, and retired offers.

## 3. Audience

- Ideal customers and buyers
- Their situation, sophistication, and technical level
- Three important pains or desired outcomes
- What they already tried
- Buying objections and decision triggers
- People the business does not serve

## 4. Voice and communication

- Three to five real writing samples from posts, emails, ads, messages, or pages
- Desired tone and preferred length
- Words, phrases, spellings, formatting, and sign-off used consistently
- Words, promises, clichés, or tones to avoid
- Differences between channels or audiences
- Languages and right-to-left requirements

## 5. Beliefs and boundaries

- What the user believes creates results
- Popular advice or industry behavior they reject
- Ethical, legal, brand, or reputation boundaries
- Actions Claude must always take or never take
- Decisions requiring explicit approval

## 6. Operations and tools

- Platforms and tools used, and what each one is for
- Important repositories, folders, documents, dashboards, and public URLs
- Source of truth for projects, tasks, customers, or content
- Recurring workflows and handoffs
- Non-secret account, list, project, or workspace IDs only when operationally useful

Never collect credentials or authentication data.

## 7. Current work

- Current priorities and what "done" means
- Active projects, status, next step, deadline, and owner
- Important decisions already made
- Known blockers and dependencies
- How progress should be reported

## 8. Repeated tasks and future skills

For three to five recurring tasks:

- Trigger or request
- Inputs and source material
- Expected output
- Quality bar and approval point
- Example of a good result
- Common failure or forbidden behavior

Flag tasks that are stable and repeatable as future skill candidates.

## 9. Proof and authority

- Years of relevant experience
- Customers served or other verified scale
- Results the user may accurately claim
- Testimonials or case studies
- Competitors or alternatives
- Differentiators and named methodology

Record evidence sources and avoid unsupported claims.

## 10. Optional personal context

Ask permission before this section. Useful topics may include:

- Roles outside the business and important areas of life
- Working hours, energy patterns, routines, and communication preferences
- Learning style and decision-making style
- Accessibility needs
- People and relationships relevant to scheduling or work
- Personal goals the user explicitly wants Claude to support
- Topics that should remain private or never be stored

Avoid intrusive questions. Do not collect sensitive information merely because it might be useful someday.

## 11. Examples

When available, collect or link to:

- A successful ad
- A successful email
- A representative post or script
- A strong proposal, landing page, or deliverable
- A poor example and why it failed

Store long examples outside the root `CLAUDE.md` and link to them.
